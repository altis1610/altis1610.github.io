AphaSpeak — Multimodal Communication Assistance for Aphasia

ISEF 2026 Finalist TISF First Award 3rd, National Science Fair Microsoft Young Hero Corning Innovation Award
POSTER (PDF) ↗

PROBLEM

Aphasia from brain damage creates severe communication barriers. Existing AAC tools rely on sequential symbol selection — slow, cognitively heavy, and unable to cover real-world scenarios. Text-only LLM approaches work on clean, human-annotated transcripts; in real clinical use, slurred speech makes raw ASR output broken and ambiguous, and a single modality misses the cues needed to recover intent.

APPROACH

AphaSpeak fuses three streams into one LLM prompt: speech representation — Whisper ASR cross-verified with ViSpeR lip-reading to resolve ambiguous phonemes; expression behavior — ORB-selected keyframes feeding gesture and emotion detection (Xception), plus a filmstrip of actions; and contextual & scene information — ambient-audio transcripts, scene images, and patient habits summarized from history for personalized pronunciation correction. A concise chain-of-thought fusion produces ranked candidate sentences the patient confirms with one tap.

SYSTEMS ENGINEERING

The first prototype took 18 seconds per utterance — unusable in conversation. I rebuilt the pipeline around asynchronous WebSocket streaming, a three-tier priority task queue, and multi-process model isolation to work around the Python GIL, bringing end-to-end latency under 4 seconds — close to natural conversational pace — on low-cost hardware.
