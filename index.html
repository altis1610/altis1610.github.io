A phone-based system that reconstructs what a person with Broca's or transcortical motor aphasia is trying to say. It fuses speech, lip-based visual speech recognition (ViSpeR), gestures and facial expression, scene images, and conversation history into ranked candidate sentences the user can pick and speak aloud.

  • Post-training: designed a choice-filtered distillation recipe — inject the reference sentence into the candidate list, re-rank by semantic similarity, drop low-quality samples — then fine-tuned a 14B model with QLoRA. The student beats its Gemini 2.5 Flash teacher by ~4% on this task.
  • Data augmentation: simulated aphasic speech by using attention scores to keep each sentence's semantic skeleton and degrade secondary words, matching clinical corpora far better than random masking.
  • Systems: cut latency from 18s to under 4s with async WebSocket streaming, three-tier priority queues, multiprocessing around Python's GIL, and CTC greedy decoding with mixed precision for the AVSR path.
  • IRB-supervised pilot with a regional hospital in progress.

Inversion attacks and defenses on speaker classification models

2023 – 2025 · first author

WISPO 2025 — Silver Medal (Bali, Indonesia)  ·  Taiwan Int'l Science Fair 2025 — Third Award
64th National Science Fair — Honorable Mention  ·  D-Link Smart Living Innovation Award

Gradient-based model inversion can recover a speaker's vocal traits from a trained classifier — raw material for voice deepfakes. We measured how architecture shapes that vulnerability and when differential privacy actually helps.

  • Trained speaker classifiers over three input pipelines — raw waveform, SincNet, and MFCC — on three public datasets; implemented both whole-clip and sliding-window inversion attacks.
  • Findings: MFCC front-ends are the most vulnerable and raw-waveform end-to-end models the most robust; attacks cost the most on 1.6–2.0s inputs; DP-SGD roughly halves attack success on raw/SincNet models but does little for MFCC.
  • When a GAN-based attack stalled with oscillating loss, ran a loss-sensitivity diagnosis in parallel with a simpler constrained-optimization track — direct gradient refinement proved more stable and became the final method.
