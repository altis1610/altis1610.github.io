Deep-Learning Reading Group

A twice-weekly study group for CS-track juniors; I designed and taught the eight AI sessions.

How the group is set up

Hualien has few CS resources, and juniors at our school who wanted to get into programming or AI had no one to learn from. In our final semester, three of us seniors — Chih-Jui Yang, Yu-Jui Shen, and I — turned what we each knew best into a course for the CS-track sophomores, meeting twice a week for a full semester.

The course ran in two parts in parallel. Yang and Shen taught competitive programming: complexity, recursion, DFS/BFS, greedy, binary search, STL, graph theory, DP, union-find. I designed and taught the AI curriculum.

The eight AI sessions

  • Overview: what a neural network actually computes
  • Environment: Linux commands, SSH, national TWCC compute, first PyTorch projects
  • Convolution and its use cases
  • The Transformer architecture
  • Common language models and ViT applications
  • Contrastive learning and agentic harnesses
  • Reinforcement learning, part 1
  • Reinforcement learning, part 2

Two design decisions: every session includes the math — gradient descent, backpropagation, parameter updates — before any framework code; and the environment session comes second, so students could run their own experiments on TWCC within the first month.

What I got out of it

My standard for a prepared session: I can rebuild the full derivation on a whiteboard without notes. Questions I could not answer cleanly showed me exactly what I had not understood, and I went back and fixed those first.
