2024 – 26 · TEACHING

Teaching Lead, InfoSec Research Club

Designed and taught the club's cryptographic-protocol lecture series from sophomore year.

How I got in

The summer before high school I watched a video introducing the Enigma machine and oblivious transfer, started reading what papers I could handle, and joined the school's Information Security Research Club. In sophomore year I became its teaching lead.

The lecture series

The series covered oblivious transfer, double encryption, and the millionaires' problem. Each protocol was built into an interactive simulation: members executed every step themselves, and for the millionaires' problem we walked a concrete case end to end, checking the security condition at each step.

How I prepare a lesson

  • Abstract the core problem and its input/output conditions
  • Modularize the protocol into sub-steps that can be analyzed independently
  • Verify the full structure end to end before presenting it

From the club to research

In sophomore year my advisor suggested applying the attack-and-defense mindset to AI models. I picked speaker-recognition models as the target, and that project became my first-author research on model-inversion attacks.
