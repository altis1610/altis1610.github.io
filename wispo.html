Analysis of Inversion Attack and Defense Strategies on Speaker Classification Models

WISPO 2025 Silver TISF Third Award Honorable Mention, National Science Fair D-Link Innovation Award
FULL PAPER (PDF) ↗

PROBLEM

Deepfake voice scams need representative speaker features — and model inversion attacks can reconstruct them directly from a trained recognition model, recovering timbre, rhythm, and accent. Prior work rarely asked how the model's architecture itself governs this vulnerability. As first author I led the research design and core experiments answering exactly that.

METHOD

We attacked three target families — MFCC pipelines, SincNet with learnable sinc filters, and raw-waveform CNNs — with white-box, gradient-based inversion: iteratively refining a synthetic input until the model's output matches a target speaker, under traditional and sliding masking algorithms. Defense used a post-training differential-privacy layer (top-k sampling with the exponential mechanism). When our GAN-based attack oscillated, I ran two parallel tracks — diagnosing the GAN vs. reformulating as constrained gradient optimization — and let the data pick the winner, clearing the bottleneck in two weeks.

FINDINGS

Architecture is the defense

Deep raw-waveform models resist inversion best; SincNet balances convergence and defense; MFCC discards cues early and leaks easiest.

A defensive sweet spot

Input durations of 1.6–2.0 s give the best trade-off between classification performance and inversion resistance.

DP is an amplifier, not a shield

Differential privacy strengthens already-robust architectures but fails to rescue weak MFCC baselines.

Design principles

Together these establish architecture-level guidance for building privacy-preserving speaker recognition.
